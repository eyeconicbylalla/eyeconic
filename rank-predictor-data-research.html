Where the historical data actually comes from — and where it doesn't exist

A four-agent verification sweep of public datasets, official exam bodies, third-party cutoff databases and Dr Mayukh Hazra's YouTube channel, with a minimum viable dataset and a 3-day execution plan.

Evidence graded: OFFICIAL VERIFIED ESTIMATE BLOCKED / N/A

Executive verdict

  1. No single dataset exists — but ~80% of the production dataset is obtainable from official PDFs plus two verified GitHub mirrors, mostly bulk-downloadable. You do not need to build this from scratch by hand.
  2. FMGE and UPSC CMS are not rank-&-branch exams. FMGE is pass/fail (150/300, no ranks, no counselling); UPSC CMS recruits to posts, not branches. They need different features (pass-probability / selection-probability), not the same predictor with different data. This must be confirmed with the product owner.
  3. NEET PG is launchable now: the complete official score↔rank distribution for 2025 (242,493 candidates, score + rank) is a single downloadable PDF, and machine-readable 2024+2025 counselling allotments already exist on GitHub.
  4. The GT→exam-score link (relationship A) has no public ground truth anywhere — no coaching institute publishes GT calibration data. It can only be built from Eyeconic's own cohort: GT attempts are already captured; post-exam outcome capture is the missing piece to build.
  5. Do NOT start bulk manual extraction of Dr Mayukh Hazra's videos. Answer: NO (one narrow exception for 3–5 INI-CET "corrects vs AIR" videos, only if INI-CET score-based prediction is in MVP scope). Full reasoning in Part 11.

Part 3

Official sources — the primary evidence

Verified by live download/parse where tooling allowed. Gov sites 403-block many automated fetchers; the workaround noted per source is real and tested.

NEET PG · MCC counselling — OFFICIAL VERIFIED

  • Archive 2020–2025: mcc.nic.in/archive-pg (497 documents parsed; current cycle at /pg-medical; partial mirror at intramcc.nic.in). 2019 and earlier: not on MCC's estate — Wayback only. So "7 years of counselling data" is not even obtainable officially.
  • Round-wise allotment PDFs per round (R1/R2/R3/mop-up/stray), e.g. Final Allotment R1 2024: SNo | AIR | Quota | Institute | Course | Allotted Category | Candidate Category | Remarks. Text-extractable (not scanned), 2–10 MB, thousands of rows. No marks, no precomputed closing ranks — derived by aggregation.
  • Seat matrices 2021–2025 per quota; admitted-candidate lists (e.g. 2024 admitted list).
  • Bulk download: realistic — static HTML index + direct CDN PDFs + an incidental WordPress REST API for machine harvesting.

NEET PG results · NBEMS — OFFICIAL VERIFIED LIVE

  • The single most valuable file found in this investigation: NEET PG 2025 Notice Board Result — 4,850 pages, all 242,493 candidates with Total Score /800 + NEET-PG Rank. This is the complete empirical score→rank distribution. No model needed — a lookup.
  • 2021–2024 equivalents existed (2024 mirrored on Scribd with percentile+rank) but their URLs are tokenized behind the site's Turnstile gate — retrievable via a real browser session or Wayback CDX; direct-PDF URLs still download despite the gate (verified).
  • Category cutoff notices per year (e.g. 2025: Gen 276 → revised to 103 in Jan 2026) — official anchor points.
  • FMGE (NBEMS): pass mark 150/300; Performance Reports (country/institute-wise) verified only for 2015–2019 via Wayback; per-session results are login+captcha lookups; no candidate-level score distribution is published, ever.

INI-CET · AIIMS — OFFICIAL VERIFIED

  • Result PDFs per session (e.g. INICET_July_23_Result-NET.pdf, 606 pp): all qualified candidates, Roll | Category | PwBD | Overall Rank | Percentile. Naming pattern covers sessions back to 2021 (INI-CET's first year). No marks — AIIMS has never published INI-CET marks.
  • Counselling allotment PDFs per session × round (mock/1st/2nd/open/spot), e.g. RESULT_OPEN_ROUND_INICET_JAN_2025: Roll | Overall Rank | Category | PwBD | Allotted Specialty | Allotted Institute | Roster point, all INIs (AIIMS×N, JIPMER, PGIMER, NIMHANS, SCTIMST). Verified live for Jan 2022, 2023, 2025, Jul 2025 sessions. Jan 2021 → Jul 2026 coverage.
  • Access quirk: the portal is now a JS SPA; but the document host docs.aiimsexams.ac.in serves PDFs directly (verified HTTP 200 for 2023–2025 files). Filename enumeration via Wayback CDX.

UPSC CMS — OFFICIAL VERIFIED — cleanest source of all four

  • Cutoff PDFs, 13 consecutive years (2013–2025), category-wise, written (/500) and final (/600): e.g. CMSE 2025, CMSE 2024. Index: upsc.gov.in/examinations/cutoff-marks.
  • Marks of recommended candidates (2021/2022/2024/2025 verified): rank-ordered Written | PT | Final | Community | PwBD — partial marks→selection data (recommended candidates only).
  • No bot-gate; stable direct URLs; trivial bulk download.

Part 5

Three relationships, three different data realities

These are not one problem. Each link has its own data availability, confidence and methodology — and one of them (A) has no public ground truth at all.

A — GT corrects → expected exam score

gt_percentile → exam_percentile → exam_score
CONFIDENCE: LOW (until our cohort data accrues)
  • Required: paired data — same student's GT attempts + actual exam outcome.
  • Available publicly: none. No coaching platform publishes GT calibration. Hazra's bands are coarse, anecdotal and self-disclaimed.
  • Best source: Eyeconic's own app (GT attempts already recorded) + an outcome-capture form we must build.
  • Methodology: start with a percentile-transfer prior (a student's percentile within a GT cohort ≈ their exam percentile, resolved to a score/rank via the official distribution), then recalibrate empirically as paired outcomes accumulate. Show wide intervals; label as estimate.

B — exam score → predicted rank

empirical quantile lookup on official distribution
NEET PG: HIGH · UPSC: MEDIUM · INI-CET: LOW-MED
  • NEET PG: solved — the Notice-Board PDF (242,493 rows, 2025) is the mapping; older years retrievable. Handle the 800→720 scale break with per-year anchors.
  • INI-CET: marks never published (official). Only rank+percentile. Use crowd-sourced corrects→AIR ladders (Hazra) as an explicitly-labeled estimate, or make rank/percentile the primary input.
  • UPSC CMS: cutoffs + recommended-candidates marks give a partial distribution — selection-probability framing, not a rank curve.
  • Methodology: monotonic empirical-quantile interpolation per exam-year; no parametric fitting needed at n=242k.

C — predicted rank → possible branches

closing-rank threshold table per year × round × quota × category
CONFIDENCE: HIGH
  • Required: closing rank per institute × course × category × quota × round.
  • Available: official allotment PDFs (MCC 2020–25; AIIMS 2021–26) — derivable by aggregation; prebuilt 2024+2025 CSVs on GitHub for day one.
  • Methodology: threshold lookup with year-over-year range (min–max across available years), filtered by user category; output "safe / likely / reach" bands rather than a single branch list.
  • Reverse direction (Desired Branch Predictor): same table inverted — branch → historical closing-rank range → required rank → required score (via B) → required GT performance (via A). Free feature once C+B exist.
Part 6

The "recent 3 GTs" ambiguity — options, not a decision

The spoken discussion (min of recent 3) and the written spec (3–5 GTs, averaged/weighted) conflict. This is a product-owner decision; below is what each option requires and how defensible it is.

MethodologyLogicHistorical data it requiresStatistical defensibility
Min of last 3 GTsPessimistic floor — "assume you'll perform like your worst recent day"Validation that the min predicts final scores better than the mean — i.e. paired GT/outcome data we do not haveWeak as an estimator (uses 1 of 3 observations, discards information); defensible only as a displayed conservative scenario
Mean of last 3–5Standard noise reduction across test instancesNone to deploy; paired data to validateStrongest default — averaging repeated measurements is textbook psychometrics; robust to one bad test day
Weighted / exponential decayRecent GTs matter more (improvement trend)Enough per-student GT history to fit decay — our app accumulates thisDefensible refinement once ≥5 GTs per student exist; premature now

Ask the product owner (blocking questions)
  • Min, mean, or both displayed as scenarios (recommended framing: "likely / conservative")?
  • 3 or 5 GTs as the input window? Are GTs from different platforms (Marrow/PrepLadder/ours) mixable, or Eyeconic-only?
  • If a student has fewer than 3 GTs — refuse prediction, or widen intervals?
Part 7

"With Eyeconic" vs "Without" — what would make it defensible

The claim "With Eyeconic your predicted score improves by X%" is a causal claim. Causal claims need a measured cohort.

What exists today (verified in this repo)

The legacy page client/src/pages/GtPredictor.tsx already implements this as GT_SCORE_TABLE — hardcoded multipliers from +15% to +150% by band and time-to-exam, capped at 180 corrects. These numbers have no data behind them — they are a product assumption expressed in code. That is legitimate for marketing if labeled as such, but it is not a prediction.

  • To be scientifically defensible you need: students' GT trajectory while using Eyeconic + their actual exam outcome + a baseline/control (non-users or pre-usage GTs), enough to isolate the program's effect from natural improvement. We have none of this. Neither does anyone else — no public dataset contains mentorship-program uplift.
  • Can we legitimately compute an improvement factor today? No. Any percentage would be invented. (Instructed not to invent one; we won't.)
  • Correct MVP posture: keep it a configurable product assumption — admin-editable multipliers, visually labeled "illustrative", not "predicted".
  • The path to making it real (cheap to start now): the app already records GT attempts. Add (1) a post-exam outcome form ("what was your final score/rank?") and (2) program-start GT baseline per student. Within 1–2 exam cycles, measured uplift replaces the table — the same dataset that calibrates relationship A.

Part 9

Minimum viable dataset vs production dataset

Based on actual availability and statistical need — not a guess. Key statistical fact: relationship B needs only one full-cohort distribution per exam-year (n=242,493 for NEET PG 2025) for a precise quantile mapping; additional years buy drift detection, not precision. Relationship C is where year-count matters, because closing ranks shift with seat matrices and exam difficulty.

Do we need 7 years? No — and mostly we can't have them.

Official counselling coverage starts 2020 (MCC) and 2021 (INI-CET); Hazra's data starts 2024; the exam itself changed materially (pattern change ~2024, the 2025 normalization controversy, the 800→720 scale break in 2026) making pre-2021 years less representative, not more. Recommended: 2–3 recent cycles for MVP, backfill to 2020/2021 for production. Seven years is neither available nor statistically necessary.
